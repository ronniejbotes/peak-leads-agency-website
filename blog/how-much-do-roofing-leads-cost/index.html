Lead costs

How much do roofing leads cost in 2026?

Bradley Hart, Peak Leads · July 30, 2026

Shared roofing leads cost $15-60 each, and the same homeowner is sold to 3-5 roofers. Exclusive leads cost $41-150 and go to one contractor only. The number to watch is cost per booked job, and that depends more on speed and follow-up than on the sticker price.

Lead pricing is the most common question we get from roofers. It is also the most common place they get burned. Vendors quote a price per lead but rarely explain who else bought it, how old it is, or what it turns into once you start dialing. This guide lays out the 2026 numbers by source so you can compare offers on the same footing. You do not need to hire us to use any of it.

What do roofing leads cost at a glance?

Typical roofing lead costs by source, 2026
Source Typical price per lead Who else gets it
Shared marketplaces (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Networx) $15-60 3-5 contractors
Exclusive lead vendors $41-150 You only
Google Local Services Ads $25-100, pay per lead You only

Every range above moves with your market, the season and the job type. A repair lead in a quiet suburb and a full replacement lead in a storm market are different products at different prices. The sections below explain each row.

What does a shared roofing lead cost?

Shared leads are the cheapest ticket in. On marketplaces like Angi, HomeAdvisor and Networx, a roofing lead typically runs $15-60 depending on your market and the job. Repairs sit at the low end. Full replacements in a metro market sit at the top.

The catch is in the word shared. That same homeowner is sold to 3-5 contractors, often within minutes. You are not buying a customer. You are buying a spot in a race. The homeowner filled in one form and now five roofers are calling. The first company on the phone usually gets the conversation, and the other four paid for the privilege of dialing.

That does not make shared leads a scam. It makes them a specific tool. If someone on your team can call within five minutes, every time, a $30 shared lead can still pencil out. If your crew calls back at 7pm, most of that money is gone before you pick up the phone. We break down the full economics in exclusive vs shared leads.

What does an exclusive roofing lead cost?

Exclusive leads run $41-150. One lead, one buyer. Nobody else gets the homeowner's number.

The spread is wide because exclusivity is only half the price. The other half is quality: how the lead was generated, how it was verified, and how fast it reaches you. A form fill scraped from a generic national site is worth less than a homeowner who contacted a branded local page about their own roof.

Ask every vendor two questions. Where did this lead come from? And does it stay exclusive forever, or only for the first 24 hours? Some vendors resell after a window. That is a shared lead with a head start, and it should be priced like one.

What do Google Local Services Ads cost?

Local Services Ads sit at the very top of Google, above the regular ads. You pay per lead rather than per click, and the Google Guaranteed badge comes with the program. For roofing, lead prices commonly land between $25 and $100 depending on the market.

LSA has taken a bigger share of local searches every year, and more contractors now compete inside it. Your ranking depends heavily on reviews and how fast you answer the phone, not just budget. You can dispute clearly bad leads, but expect to spend time on it. For many roofers this is the best first paid channel, because intent is high and you only pay when someone actually reaches out. It is one of the channels we manage under paid ads.

What does it cost to generate your own leads?

Running your own campaigns costs more up front and usually wins over time, because every lead is exclusive by definition and carries your brand.

Google Ads. Roofing clicks are among the most expensive in home services. Expect $6-25 per click in most markets, more where storm restoration budgets compete. Whether that becomes a $60 lead or a $250 lead depends almost entirely on your landing page. A slow site that hides the phone number will double your cost per lead before the auction does. That is why we treat web design and ad management as one system, not two invoices.

Meta ads. Facebook and Instagram leads are cheaper, often $20-80, but colder. These people were not searching for a roofer. Demand generation works, but it needs a clear offer and fast follow-up. We compare the two channels properly in Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for contractors.

SEO. The slow channel. Months of work before it pays, then it compounds. Once you rank in the local pack, the incremental cost of each lead falls toward zero, which no ad channel ever does. See what that work involves under SEO for contractors.

Why is cost per lead the wrong number?

Cost per lead is what vendors sell on. Cost per booked job is what you bank on.

Here is a worked example with round numbers. Say you have $800 to spend.

  • Shared: 20 leads at $40. You reach 8 before a competitor closes the door, quote 5, book 2. That is $400 per booked job.
  • Exclusive: 8 leads at $100. You reach 6, quote 5, book 3. That is $267 per booked job.

Your rates will differ, and some high-volume roofers beat these numbers on shared leads. The point is the shape of the math. Contact rate and close rate move the real cost far more than the sticker price does.

The other lever is speed. Homeowners tend to hire whoever calls first, not whoever calls best. Whatever you buy, get the first call happening within minutes and the same leads suddenly cost less per job.

What moves the price of a roofing lead?

  • Your market. Metro areas with strong roofing demand price higher than rural counties.
  • Season and storms. After a hail event, lead prices in the affected zip codes can double while every restoration company piles in.
  • Job type. Replacement and storm damage leads cost more than repair leads because the job value is higher.
  • Freshness. A lead generated an hour ago is worth several times one recycled from last month's list.
  • Verification. A lead with a confirmed phone number, address and stated intent costs more and wastes less of your time.

What should you do with these numbers?

Compare every offer on cost per booked job, not cost per lead. Ask who else gets the lead and when it was generated. Get your first call happening within minutes. And before you spend anything, make sure the place the lead lands, your website and your phone process, can actually convert it.
