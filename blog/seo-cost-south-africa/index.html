How much does SEO cost in South Africa?

30 October 2026

What SEO costs in South Africa depends on how much work your business needs to get found, and an honest agency can't price that before it has looked. If you run a trade business, the monthly fee mostly comes down to how many services and areas you want to show up for, how hard your trade is to rank for where you work, what state your website is in, and who writes the pages. A quote that names each of those is one you can hold up against the next agency's.

You've probably had SEO quotes that are miles apart, with no clear reason why. "SEO" is a label on a monthly invoice, and what sits behind it can be a lot of work or almost none. When you know what moves the fee, you can tell which kind of quote you're holding.

Why SEO quotes are so hard to compare

An SEO quote can be a monthly amount and a short list of words like "optimisation", "content" and "reporting". Those words can cover real work on your site every month, or an automated report nobody reads. You can't see the work the way you can see a new website, so it's easy to compare totals and miss what each total buys.

Google's own page for business owners, Do you need an SEO?, is blunt about this. It says nobody can guarantee a top ranking on Google, and to beware of anyone claiming guaranteed rankings or a special relationship with Google. It also says to be wary of agencies that email you out of the blue. If your quote came from a cold email promising the top of Google, you can stop comparing it now.

What moves a monthly SEO fee

How many services and areas you want to be found for

One kind of job in one area you'll travel to can be a search somebody types. "Geyser replacement Benoni" and "leak detection Boksburg" are different searches, and each can need its own page and its own work. A plumber who works a small patch of suburbs needs far less work than an installer who covers the whole country. That's the main reason a fair agency asks about your services and areas before it names a fee.

How hard your trade is to rank for where you work

Some trades in some towns have plenty of established businesses already sitting at the top. Others have very little competition. Getting past businesses that have been doing SEO for a long time takes more work than filling a gap nobody has bothered with. An agency should look at who's ranking for your searches before quoting, and tell you what it found.

What state your website is in

If your site is slow, hard to use on a phone, or puts every service on the home page, an SEO fee pays to fix that first. Sometimes that's sensible. Sometimes rebuilding the site first costs less than paying an agency to drag the old site up the rankings. Google itself says a great time to bring in an SEO is when you're thinking about a redesign or planning a new site, and the earlier the better. If a new site is on the cards, what a trade website costs explains that side of the bill.

Who writes the pages

Pages that win searches say something true and specific about the jobs you do, ideally next to photos you took on site. That writing takes time. If the agency does it, ask how they'll get to know your jobs. If the answer doesn't involve talking to you, expect wording that could sit on anyone's site. Doing it yourself should bring the fee down, though the writing then comes out of your own time.

Your Google Business Profile

If your customers look for trades like yours on Google Maps, profile work can be part of the fee: categories, services, photos and reviews. Google says plainly that there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. What it weighs is how well your profile matches the search, how far you are from the person searching, and how well known your business is. So you're paying for work on those things, never for a position.

What an SEO fee should never pay for

Some things show up on cheap quotes because they're quick to do and look busy on a report. Google's spam policies name buying links for ranking purposes as link spam. They also name generating many pages mainly to manipulate rankings, such as a near-identical page for every town in the province. Both can do your site harm that you then pay someone else to undo. Google's own advice is to research the damage an irresponsible SEO can do before you hire.

If a quote mentions "backlink packages" or promises a page for every town, ask exactly what those are and where they'll come from.

Questions to ask before you compare SEO quotes

Put the same questions to every agency, and compare what each one says:

  • Which services and areas is the fee for, by name?
  • What work happens each month, and how will I see it was done?
  • Who writes the pages, and will you interview me about my work?
  • Is Google Business Profile work included, and what does it cover?
  • Will you fix technical problems on the site, or only list them?
  • Do you follow Google Search Essentials, and how do you get links?
  • What results should I expect, and how will you measure them?
  • Will my business own the Search Console account, with you added as a user?
  • Can you show me work you've done for businesses like mine?
  • What happens if I want to stop?

Some of these come straight from Google's own list of questions to ask an SEO. The Search Console question matters more than it looks. Search Console is where Google shows which searches your site appears for, and only an owner of the property can add or remove users. What should an SEO report include each month? covers what you should then be sent.

It's also fair to ask yourself whether you need an agency yet. Google says a small local business can probably do much of the work itself. If you work a small patch and the phone already rings, setting up your profile properly and getting your name and number right everywhere may be enough for now.
