Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for contractors

Bradley Hart, Peak Leads · July 30, 2026

Google Ads reaches people searching for your trade right now, so leads cost more but close faster. Facebook ads reach people who were not looking, so leads are cheaper but colder. Run Google first for steady booked jobs. Use Meta to fill slow weeks and stay visible in your area.

Both channels work. They do different jobs, and confusing the two is the most expensive mistake we see contractors make with ad budgets. This guide covers what each channel costs in 2026, what each is actually for, and the order we would spend in if it were our money.

What is the real difference?

Google Ads is demand capture. Someone types "roof repair near me" because water is coming through the ceiling. Your ad meets a problem that already exists. Intent is high, and so is the price of the click.

Facebook and Instagram ads are demand generation. Nobody opens Facebook looking for a plumber. Your ad interrupts someone scrolling, plants your name, and sometimes catches a homeowner who has been putting the decision off. Intent is low, reach is cheap.

One sentence version: Google finds people looking for a contractor. Facebook finds people scrolling past one. You are not choosing which is better. You are choosing which job needs doing this quarter.

What does Google Ads cost for contractors?

Home services clicks are expensive because the jobs are valuable. Roofing sits near the top: expect $6-25 per click in most US markets, and more where storm restoration budgets compete for the same searches. Plumbing, HVAC and electrical typically land in a similar band.

Cost per lead is where campaigns are won or lost, and it depends less on the auction than on your landing page. The same $15 clicks become $60 leads on a page that loads fast, shows the phone number and proves you are local, or $250 leads on a slow homepage that hides the number below three paragraphs about your founding story. Typical contractor cost per lead runs $50-250 across trades and markets.

The upside justifies the price. Search leads are exclusive to you, carry your brand from the first click, and close at higher rates than any purchased lead, because the homeowner picked you before you ever spoke. This is the channel we build first under paid ads, and we treat the landing page as part of the campaign, because it is.

What do Facebook and Instagram ads cost?

Meta leads typically cost $20-80 for home services, often through in-app lead forms rather than your website. That price buys volume, not intent.

The leads behave differently. A Meta lead filled in a form between two videos. Call within minutes and you have a real conversation. Call the next day and many will not remember submitting anything. Meta campaigns live or die on three things: a concrete offer, creative that shows real work, and follow-up speed.

What Meta does well is fill gaps. When the calendar has holes in it, a financing offer or a seasonal inspection promo to homeowners in your service area generates real appointments at a cost per lead Google cannot match. It just takes more calls to turn those leads into jobs. The same exclusivity math from exclusive vs shared leads applies here in your favor: every lead your own ads generate belongs to you alone.

Where do Local Services Ads fit?

Local Services Ads are Google's pay-per-lead program. Your business appears at the very top of search with a Google Guaranteed badge, and you pay $25-100 per lead instead of paying per click, with the price set by trade and market.

LSA keeps taking a bigger share of local search, and for many trades it is now the first paid dollar worth spending. Ranking depends on reviews, response speed and proximity more than budget, so a contractor with 200 good reviews can outrank a bigger spender. The catches: lead quality disputes take time, call volume can be uneven, and you have little control over targeting. Run it alongside search, not instead of it.

Which should you run first?

Budget decides. These are the sequences we would run with our own money:

  • Under $1,000 a month. LSA plus a tightly matched search campaign on your highest-value service. Nothing else. A small budget split three ways produces three sets of noise.
  • $2,000-5,000 a month. Search and LSA carry the load. Add retargeting on Meta so homeowners who visited your site keep seeing your name for pennies.
  • Above that, or a seasonal trade. Add Meta prospecting with a real offer to smooth the slow months, and keep search running through peak season when everyone else bids up.

When do Facebook ads beat Google?

Meta wins in specific situations, and it is worth knowing them rather than treating it as a cheap Google.

  • Filling slow weeks with an offer, because you can generate demand on demand.
  • Visual trades. Before-and-after roofing, painting and landscaping creative stops thumbs.
  • New markets where nobody searches your name yet and you need local awareness.
  • Financing and promo offers that give a scrolling homeowner a reason to act now.
  • Retargeting. The cheapest ads you will ever run are the ones shown to people who already visited your site.

How long before the ads pay for themselves?

Set expectations before you start, because most contractor ad accounts are abandoned in the exact week they were about to work.

Search and LSA produce leads within days, but the first month's cost per lead is not the real number. It takes 4-8 weeks of cutting wasted keywords, testing ad copy and tightening the landing page before the account settles into its true cost. Judge the channel at 90 days, on booked jobs and revenue, not on week two's lead count.

Budget for enough data to judge with. At $100 per lead, a $500 monthly budget buys five leads, and five of anything proves nothing. If you cannot fund a channel to at least 15-20 leads a month, run one channel properly instead of two channels badly.

What do you need before you spend a dollar?

Three things, in order. First, tracking from the click to the booked call, not to the form fill. If you cannot see which keyword produced which job, you cannot cut waste. Second, a fast page with the phone number visible, because it sets your cost per lead more than bids do. Third, reviews, because every channel above leans on them.

And run the long game alongside the paid one. SEO takes months, then makes every future lead cheaper, so paid traffic becomes a choice rather than life support.
