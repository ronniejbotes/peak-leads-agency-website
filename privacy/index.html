Privacy

Privacy notice

Peak Leads collects your name, email address, phone number and whatever you type into a form on this website, and uses those details to reply to you about the work you asked about. Nothing is sold and nothing is passed to anyone who is not listed on this page. To have your details removed, email bradley@peakleads.agency and ask.

Who is asking for your details?

Peak Leads, a South African web design, SEO, paid advertising and lead generation agency. The address for anything on this page, including a request to see or delete what is held about you, is bradley@peakleads.agency.

What information does this website collect?

Three forms on this site ask for personal details, and each one asks only for what it needs to reply to you:

  • The contact form on the contact page asks for your name, email address, an optional phone number and your message.
  • The free audit at /free-audit/ asks eight questions: your name, business email, contact number, business name, trade, the service you are interested in, your current monthly advertising spend and your website address.
  • The hidden mini game asks for a name and an email address if you want your score on its scoreboard. Skipping that prompt still records the score, without a name.

Booking a call opens a Calendly calendar embedded in the page. Whatever you type into that calendar goes to Calendly and comes back to us as a booking.

Why is it collected?

To answer you. A form on this site is a request for a reply, a quote or a call, and your details are used to give you one. They are also used to keep a record of what was discussed if you go on to become a client. They are not used to send marketing you did not ask for, and they are not sold or rented to anyone.

Who else receives your information?

Peak Leads does not run its own mail server, calendar or advertising measurement. Four outside companies are involved in this website working, and each one sees a different slice:

What is the Meta tracking code doing?

This site loads the Meta Pixel, a small piece of Meta's code that tells Meta a browser opened a page here. It starts about a second and a half after the page finishes loading, or as soon as you click or press a key, whichever happens first. It reports three things: that a page was viewed, that a call was booked, and that the free audit form was sent. None of those three carries your name, your email address or anything you typed into a form. Meta does see the address of the page you are on, your device's IP address and the cookie its own code sets, because the code is fetched from Meta's servers and any script loaded that way sees those. To stop it, use your browser's tracking protection or an ad blocker, which will block it on this site and on every other site that uses it.

What is stored on your own device?

Two things, both kept in your own browser rather than sent anywhere:

  • Your free audit answers. The funnel saves your answers as you go, so a dropped connection or a closed tab does not cost you the eight questions, and so an enquiry that fails to send can be retried on your next visit. Clearing your browser's site data for peakleads.agency deletes this.
  • Mini game scores and the music setting. The local scoreboard and whether you muted the sound.

Meta and Calendly set their own cookies through their code when it loads. Those belong to them and are covered by their own privacy policies.

How long is it kept?

Enquiries are kept for as long as they are useful to answer you and, if you become a client, for as long as the business relationship runs plus whatever period the law requires records to be held. Anything older than that, or anything you ask us to delete, is deleted. There is no automatic purge running on a schedule, so the practical answer is: ask, and it goes.

How do you get your details corrected or deleted?

Email bradley@peakleads.agency and say what you want done. You can ask what is held about you, ask for it to be corrected, ask for it to be deleted, or object to it being used at all. You do not have to give a reason, and asking costs you nothing. South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act gives you those rights, and you can also complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa if you are not satisfied with the answer.

How will you know if this notice changes?

The date below changes whenever the wording does. If something material changes for people who have already sent an enquiry, they are told directly rather than left to notice a new date on a page.
